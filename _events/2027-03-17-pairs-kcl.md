A multi-stakeholder all day event to look for concrete ways to support democracy, human judgement, and human agency in the context of increasingly pervasive AI, is being organised by the [Centre for Data Futures (King’s College London)](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/research/centre-for-data-futures) in collaboration with the Informational Democracy working group of the Max Planck Institute.

In the morning we will be organising a session alongside [PAIRS](https://pairs.site/) partners.

<!--more-->

This workshop session will draw upon [PAIRS Public AI Voice and Engagement (PAVE) Case Book](https://pave.pairs.site), and ongoing work to develop a [citizens track in AI governance](https://citizens-track.org/) to explore different methods of participatory public engagement in the democratic governance of AI, whilst questioning what it takes for public voice to lead to substantive change. 

Using examples of participatory public engagement around the world drawn from the case book, and mapping out sources of AI governance power, discussions will centre on the interventions needed for public input to be both legitimate and effective in reshaping AI.

Participants will work in small groups to explore a range of examples of participatory engagement with AI and to identify both design features of participation processes, and institutional architectures (in local government, national government, industry, etc.) that may increase the chance of participatory engagement leading to more democratic outcomes from AI.
